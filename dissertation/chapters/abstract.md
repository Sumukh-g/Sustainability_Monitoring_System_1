# Abstract

Data-centre operators require timely evidence about energy, water, cooling and carbon performance, yet operational telemetry is often fragmented across monitoring and analytical tools. This dissertation presents the design, implementation and evaluation of an AI-Based Sustainability Monitoring System: a reproducible decision-support artefact combining sustainability key performance indicators (KPIs), demand forecasting, multivariate anomaly detection and rule-based recommendations within a multi-page Streamlit dashboard. The work followed Design Science Research and evaluated the artefact through comparative machine-learning experiments, KPI verification and functional testing.

Evaluation used 17,568 synthetic hourly observations representing two UK sites throughout 2024, with 37 variables and 288 reproducibly injected anomalies. Leakage-safe lagged and rolling features and a chronological 80/20 split were applied. HistGradientBoosting was the strongest tested forecaster for all three targets. It achieved root mean squared errors of 17.41 kWh for total facility energy, 10.44 kW for cooling demand and 21.37 litres for water consumption, improving upon previous-day baselines by 71.89%, 62.44% and 39.82%, respectively. Mean PUE, WUE and CUE were 1.23, 0.33 L/kWh and 0.31 kg CO2e/kWh. Isolation Forest detected 440 observations but achieved only 0.191 precision, 0.292 recall and 0.231 F1, demonstrating that useful forecasting performance does not imply dependable anomaly diagnosis.

All 12 automated tests and 19 project-validation checks passed. The resulting artefact provides coherent and reproducible sustainability decision support, but not autonomous control or demonstrated environmental savings. Its principal limitations are synthetic telemetry, injected anomaly labels and recommendations that have not been physically validated. Real-site evaluation, calibrated anomaly thresholds and participant usability research are therefore required before operational deployment.
